Why does my dog bark when I leave? How to tell what's behind it

By Devid Egli, Casa Scripta

Last updated 21 November 2026

How this was written: researched from the sources listed below and drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by Devid. Not reviewed by a vet.

You pick up your keys, step outside, and before the door clicks shut, it starts. Barking. Maybe howling. Your neighbor mentions it, or you catch it on a doorbell camera. So you wonder: why does my dog bark when I leave? The answer matters. Barking when you go and barking at the mail carrier are two different problems. They need different fixes. Here's how to work out which one you have, and what to do next.

Not veterinary advice. This is general information only. Talk to your vet or a qualified behavior professional about your dog. In an emergency, contact your nearest emergency vet.

Why does my dog bark when I leave?

Dogs bark when you leave for a few different reasons. Researchers often say "separation-related problems" rather than "separation anxiety." That's because dogs can bark, chew or have accidents when alone for different reasons. Anxiety is one. Frustration, boredom and reacting to outside noises are others (de Assis et al. 2020; Lenkei et al. 2021).

Dogs Trust puts it simply: dogs "that are worried or frustrated about being left alone may bark, howl, or whine when you leave them." It adds that these signs "might start when you're getting ready to leave, or only happen once you're already out" (Dogs Trust, barking).

One study found a link between how dogs react when left and how they handle other frustrating or scary situations. Dogs with more fear-related traits tended to whine rather than bark. Dogs that scored higher on a trait the authors link to frustration barked more and were more likely to scratch at the door (Lenkei et al. 2021). That's one study, so treat it as a clue, not a rule.

Whatever the reason, your dog isn't doing it to get back at you. The ASPCA says anxious behaviors "are not the result of disobedience or spite. They are distress responses!" (ASPCA).

Why the barking starts so fast

If your dog starts barking the moment you leave, that's a common pattern with separation distress. The ASPCA says an anxious dog will usually start barking and showing other distress "within a short time after being left alone—often within minutes" (ASPCA). Blue Cross says "the first 15 minutes are the worst" (Blue Cross). The RSPCA says separation-related behavior shows "before or after the owner leaves, starting within a few to 30 minutes" (RSPCA).

That timing is a useful clue. So is what sets it off. The ASPCA describes separation barking as "persistent," and says it "doesn't seem to be triggered by anything except being left alone" (ASPCA).

Dog barking when left alone: distress, boredom or outside noise?

Not every dog barking when left alone has separation distress. Here's how the common causes tend to differ:

Could it be… Clues
Separation distress Starts soon after you leave; may start while you get ready; often comes with whining, pacing, panting, drooling, scratching at exits or accidents (RSPCA; ASPCA)
Barking at outside noise Barks at noises, sights or smells from outside the home; window barking is common (Dogs Trust, barking)
Boredom The ASPCA says bored dogs "usually don't appear anxious" (ASPCA)
A health problem Accidents can have medical causes, and pain or failing senses can play a part (see the vet section below)

The RSPCA lists more signs to look for. These include damage near doors or windows, accidents in the house, and a lot of excitement when you come back. Others are worry before you leave, trembling, panting, drooling, pacing and not eating while you're out (RSPCA).

Film one departure

You can't know what's going on until you see it. Dogs Trust's advice: "Spy on your dog!" It suggests setting up your phone to video, peeking through a window out of sight, or using a remote camera (Dogs Trust).

If your dog has none of the red flags below, try this:

  1. Point a camera that records sound at the door, or where your dog spends most time.
  2. Leave as you normally would, and watch live on your phone.
  3. Come back at the first sign of worry. Don't wait for full-blown howling.
  4. Write down how many seconds or minutes it took, and what you saw first.

What counts as the first sign? Dogs Trust lists whining, pacing, not settling, spending lots of time near the door, trembling, panting, drooling and losing interest in food or chews (Dogs Trust). In the book, these are on Worksheet 7: Stress Signs card, so you can circle your own dog's first sign and watch for it.

That video answers a lot. Did the barking start right away, or 30 minutes in? Was it aimed at the door or the window? That tells you which row of the table above fits best.

Why does my dog bark when I leave for work?

If the barking started when your routine changed, you're not imagining it. In a UK study of 1,807 dogs, 9.9% had developed new signs of separation-related behavior by October 2020, after COVID-19 lockdown rules eased. Dogs whose time left alone had dropped the most during lockdown were at higher risk (Harvey et al. 2022). Another study found that dogs with signs of separation problems before COVID were more likely to develop more problems during lockdown (Sherwell et al. 2023).

So a change like going back to the office may be part of the story. The RSPCA recommends not leaving a dog alone "for more than four hours; for puppies much less," and adds that a dog who struggles alone "may start feeling anxious within minutes of you leaving" (RSPCA). How to stop dog barking when left alone

There's no quick off switch, and I won't pretend there is. What helps depends on the cause.

If it's outside noise, these are simple changes worth trying:

  • Close curtains, lower blinds or use frosted film to block the view (Dogs Trust, barking).
  • Leave your dog in a quiet room, with the radio on to muffle outside sounds (RSPCA).

If it's separation distress, the fix isn't about the barking itself. It's about the fear behind it.

  • Don't leave your dog longer than they can cope with while you train. Dogs Trust says this is "the only way to stop the problem getting worse" (Dogs Trust). Its barking advice is to avoid leaving your dog until they're ready, and to ask friends, family and dog sitters to help (Dogs Trust, barking).
  • Start with very short absences. The ASPCA's main rule is to "plan your absences to be shorter than the time it takes for your dog to become upset" (ASPCA).
  • Don't punish. The ASPCA says not to scold or punish an anxious dog. Blue Cross says punishment "won't help your dog" (ASPCA; Blue Cross).
  • No bark collars. The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior says aversive methods, including electronic collars, "should not be used under any circumstances" (AVSAB). More on this in my week-one plan.

Be patient with the timeline. In one trial, after 12 weeks of treatment, 56% of owners of treated dogs reported significant improvement (Blackwell et al. 2006). Separation anxiety specialist Malena DeMartini asks owners to "think in terms of months, not weeks" (DeMartini). Results vary.

When to see your vet first

Health problems can cause or add to separation-related behavior, so the RSPCA says to visit your vet if you're concerned (RSPCA). Dogs Trust says it's important to see a vet if you think your dog has signs of separation anxiety. There may be a medical problem that's hard to spot (Dogs Trust). Pain matters too. One review looked at 100 recent dog cases sent to behavior experts. The authors' conservative estimate was that around a third involved a painful condition (Mills et al. 2020).

Call your vet before you start training if your dog:

  • hurts themselves trying to get out, such as broken teeth, cut paws or damaged nails (ASPCA)
  • tries hard to escape, especially through doors or windows (ASPCA)
  • has accidents in the house, which can have medical causes (ASPCA)
  • seems to be losing their hearing or sight (Dogs Trust)
  • walks oddly or shows other signs of pain (Mills et al. 2020)

DeMartini also recommends telling your vet, who can rule out pain or other health issues (DeMartini). Don't give any medication, supplement or calming product without talking to your vet. You can download our free Vet-First Red Flags Checklist.

FAQ

My dog only barks for a minute, then stops. Is that separation anxiety? Watch the whole video, not just the noise. Quiet isn't always calm. Signs like pacing, panting, drooling or not settling can still mean distress (Dogs Trust). If you're unsure, show the video to your vet or a qualified behavior professional.

Will a food puzzle stop the barking? It's good enrichment, but not a fix. The ASPCA notes that highly anxious dogs usually won't eat when their owners aren't home (ASPCA).

Should I go back in when my dog barks? The aim is to come back before your dog gets upset. Keep your absences shorter than the time it takes for worry to start (ASPCA).
